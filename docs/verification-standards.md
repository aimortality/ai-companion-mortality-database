# Verification Standards

> *"The difference between something that might be true and something we can stand behind."*

---

## Quick Reference

| Tier | Name | Requirement | Published? |
|------|------|-------------|------------|
| **1** | Juridical | Court documents OR government acknowledgment | ✓ Yes |
| **2** | Journalistic | 3+ independent sources OR primary document access | ✓ Yes |
| **3** | Preliminary | Single source or unverified | ✗ Tracked only |

---

## Tier 1: Juridical Evidence

The highest standard. These sources have been subjected to legal processes—sworn statements, adversarial challenge, or official governmental review.

**Qualifying sources:**

- Federal or state court filings (complaints, motions, rulings)
- Congressional or parliamentary testimony
- Regulatory body statements (FTC, state attorneys general, international DPAs)
- Law enforcement reports where AI is noted
- Coroner findings mentioning chatbot interaction
- Settlement agreements (when public)

**What this means:** If a case rests on Tier 1 evidence, someone has staked their legal or professional standing on its accuracy. Perjury, sanctions, and official misconduct attach to false statements at this level.

---

## Tier 2: Journalistic Evidence

The standard of responsible reporting. These sources have been subjected to editorial review, often legal vetting, and professional reputational stakes.

**Qualifying configurations:**

1. **Three or more independent outlets** reporting the same core facts
   - Wire service reprints do not count as independent
   - Must include at least one outlet with documented editorial standards

2. **Primary source journalism** from a single major outlet
   - Reporter reviewed chat logs directly
   - Reporter conducted original interviews with family members
   - Documentary evidence (screenshots, records) obtained and verified

3. **Platform acknowledgment**
   - Company confirms the incident occurred
   - Even without details, confirmation establishes the event

**What this means:** Professional journalists and editors have determined the information meets publication standards. Outlets risk defamation liability and reputational damage for false reporting.

---

## Tier 3: Preliminary Evidence

Insufficient for publication but sufficient for monitoring.

**Examples:**

- Single news article without independent confirmation
- Social media accounts, however detailed
- Forum posts or community reports
- Secondhand accounts from non-family members
- International cases without English-language verification

**What happens:** Cases at Tier 3 are logged internally and monitored for escalation. If additional evidence emerges, they move to Tier 2 or 1. They are never published until that threshold is met.

---

## Special Considerations

### Minors

Cases involving victims under 18 receive additional scrutiny:

- Pseudonyms used unless family has publicly identified the child
- Age verified through multiple sources
- Educational or identifying details minimized
- Family wishes regarding publicity respected where possible

### Active Litigation

For cases in ongoing legal proceedings:

- We note that allegations are unproven
- We update as rulings occur
- We distinguish between "lawsuit filed" and "court found"
- Settlements are reported without implying admission

### International Cases

Non-US cases face additional verification challenges:

- Language barriers may limit source access
- Legal systems vary in public document availability
- We note when verification is limited by jurisdiction
- International cases may require longer verification timelines

### Platform Self-Reports

When companies acknowledge incidents:

- Self-reports are weighted as Tier 2 minimum
- We note any discrepancies between company account and other sources
- Policy changes announced in response are documented

---

## What Disqualifies a Case

We do not publish cases where:

- AI interaction was incidental rather than central to events
- The only source is the platform's competitors or critics
- Family has requested non-publication and no legal record exists
- Evidence suggests misattribution or fabrication
- Harm was non-fatal and did not result in hospitalization

This is not a database of AI complaints. It is a record of verified deaths and severe harms where chatbot interaction was a documented factor.

## Escalation Path

```
Tier 3 (Monitoring)
     ↓
Additional source emerges → Cross-reference check
     ↓
Meets Tier 2 threshold? → Editorial review
     ↓
Publication with Tier 2 notation
     ↓
Court filing or government action? → Upgrade to Tier 1
```

---

## Challenging a Verification

If you believe a case has been incorrectly verified or should be reclassified:

1. **Open a GitHub issue** with "Verification Challenge" label
2. **Provide contrary evidence** with sources
3. **We review within 7 days** and respond publicly
4. **Outcome documented** in issue thread and, if warranted, commit history

We have no interest in maintaining inaccurate records. Corrections strengthen the database.

---

## The Standard We Hold Ourselves To

Before publishing any case, we ask:

*If the family of the deceased read this entry, would they recognize it as accurate and respectful?*

*If the platform's lawyers reviewed this entry, would they find it defensible as factual reporting?*

*If a researcher cited this entry, would it withstand peer review scrutiny?*

Only when all three answers are yes does a case move from internal tracking to public database.
