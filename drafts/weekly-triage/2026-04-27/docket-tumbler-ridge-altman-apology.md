## New Development (April 23–24, 2026)

### Sam Altman apology letter

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman signed and sent a personal letter of apology to the community of Tumbler Ridge, British Columbia. The letter is dated April 23, 2026 and was widely reported on April 24, 2026.

Key content of the letter (per reporting):
- Altman expressed being "deeply sorry" for OpenAI's decision not to report van Rootselaar's flagged ChatGPT activity to law enforcement in June 2025
- Acknowledged that the internal threshold requiring "the target, means, and timing" of planned violence — the standard under which van Rootselaar's account was not referred to police — was too high
- Committed to policy changes (see below)

British Columbia Premier David Eby shared the letter publicly on social media, writing: *"the apology is necessary, and yet grossly insufficient for the devastation done to the families of Tumbler Ridge."*

### OpenAI policy changes announced

Alongside the apology, OpenAI announced three voluntary policy changes:

1. **Lowered law enforcement referral threshold**: A user no longer needs to discuss "the target, means, and timing" of planned violence for a conversation to be flagged for law enforcement referral. The threshold is now broader.
2. **Direct RCMP point of contact**: OpenAI has established a dedicated contact channel with the RCMP (and presumably other law enforcement agencies, though specifics not confirmed).
3. **Behavioral expert consultation**: OpenAI has enlisted mental health and behavioral experts to help assess flagged cases before making law enforcement referral decisions.

**Critical note:** These changes are entirely voluntary and not legally binding. They can be reversed at any time and are not enforceable under current Canadian or US law.

### Ongoing investigation

The RCMP continues its review of van Rootselaar's digital activity as part of the B.C. Supreme Court proceedings (civil suit filed in 2026 by family of a critically wounded victim). No specific B.C. Supreme Court docket update was found in the April 20–27 window.

Canadian government had previously explored mandatory law enforcement reporting requirements for AI companies; no bill has been introduced as of this run.

### Canadian Parliament Hill advocacy (April 27, 2026)

Children's advocacy groups held a press conference and rally on Parliament Hill today (April 27, 2026), calling for passage of online harms legislation that would cover AI chatbots, social media, and gaming. Children First Canada (CEO Sara Erin Austin) cited the Tumbler Ridge shooting explicitly. The group is calling for duty-of-care requirements, safety-by-design mandates, and independent enforcement. The Liberal government's Culture Minister Marc Miller is reportedly developing a new bill after the previous C-63 died before the 2025 election. No bill text or timeline was announced today.
