## Summary

On April 16, 2026, University of South Florida doctoral students Zamil Limon (27) and Nahida Bristy (27), both Bangladeshi nationals, were last seen or heard from. Limon's roommate, Hisham Abugharbieh (26), was subsequently charged with two counts of first-degree murder and multiple related felonies in Hillsborough County, Florida. Both victims' remains were located; Bristy's body was found separately; items belonging to Limon were recovered from a dumpster.

Criminal court documents filed in Hillsborough County reveal that Abugharbieh asked ChatGPT on April 13 — three days before the victims were last seen — "What happens if a human has a put in a black garbage bag and thrown in a dumpster." After the victims disappeared, on April 19, he asked ChatGPT "Has there been someone who survived a sniper bullet to the head" and "Will my neighbors hear my gun." Additional ChatGPT queries concerned changing a vehicle identification number and unlicensed firearm storage.

Phone data placed Abugharbieh at the Howard Frankland Bridge twice during the early morning hours of April 17. He was ordered held without bond.

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier — who had already opened a criminal investigation into OpenAI over the April 2025 FSU mass shooting — publicly announced on April 27–28, 2026, that he was expanding the criminal investigation to include the USF murders following disclosure of Abugharbieh's ChatGPT queries.

**Mechanism note:** This case fits the instrumental_pathway taxonomy (same as the FSU and Tumbler Ridge mass shootings) but the pattern differs from those cases. In the FSU and Tumbler Ridge incidents, ChatGPT was consulted on attack planning (target selection, weapon choice, timing, media reaction). In the USF case, ChatGPT was consulted on body disposal and operational security (VIN modification, firearm noise), making it ancillary to the killings rather than a pre-attack planning tool in the same sense. The pre-murder query (April 13) does suggest pre-crime consultation. Maintainer should determine whether this meets the database's existing instrumental_pathway threshold or whether a new subtype (e.g., `post_crime_cover_up_assistance`) should be introduced.
