## Substance

Families of victims of the February 10, 2026, Tumbler Ridge, British Columbia mass shooting — in which Jesse van Rootselaar (18) killed eight people including her mother, stepbrother (age 11), five students (ages 12–13), and an educational assistant before killing herself — filed a civil lawsuit against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman in U.S. federal court in San Francisco on April 29, 2026.

**Core allegations:**
- ChatGPT "deepened the shooter's violent fixation and pushed them toward the attack — the predictable result of a design choice OpenAI made to let ChatGPT engage with users about violence in the first place"
- OpenAI's own automated system flagged van Rootselaar's account in June 2025 for "gun violence activity and planning"; a safety team reviewed the content and urged management to notify authorities; company leadership chose to deactivate the account instead of reporting it
- A human safety reviewer on OpenAI's team was alarmed enough to advocate for a law enforcement referral (per prior reporting in WSJ); the referral did not happen
- After the February 2026 shooting, OpenAI contacted RCMP with account information — OpenAI characterized this as "proactive"; critics noted it came post-massacre
- **Claims:** Negligence, wrongful death, aiding and abetting a mass shooting, product liability

**This case is distinct from the RCMP criminal investigation** (tracked in `docs/sources/court-documents.md`), which remains ongoing. This is a new civil action in U.S. federal court by victims' families.

## OpenAI responses around the same time

Separately, around the same period, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman issued an apology: *"I am deeply sorry that we did not alert law enforcement to the account that was banned in June."*

OpenAI VP Ann O'Leary informed Canadian federal ministers that the company has voluntarily lowered its reporting threshold — a user no longer needs to discuss "the target, means, and timing" of planned violence for a conversation to be flagged for law enforcement referral. OpenAI also stated it has enlisted mental health and behavioral experts and established a direct point of contact with the RCMP. These changes are voluntary, not legally binding.
