# ⚠️ 48h-rule breach — Tumbler Ridge Victim Lawsuits Filed April 29, 2026

**Filing date:** April 29, 2026
**Document type:** Wrongful death complaints (seven separate suits)
**Court:** U.S. District Court, Northern District of California, San Francisco Division
**48h-rule status:** ⚠️ BREACH — filed 12 days before this run on May 11, 2026.

## Substance

On April 29, 2026, families of victims killed in the February 10, 2026 Tumbler Ridge, British Columbia mass shooting filed seven separate wrongful death lawsuits against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman in U.S. federal court (Northern District of California, San Francisco). The suits seek damages exceeding $1 billion (USD).

**Background:** The Tumbler Ridge shooting is already tracked in the database as incident `2026-02-CA-001` (Jesse van Rootselaar, 18, killed 8 people including his mother and 5 students aged 12–13 at a school in Tumbler Ridge, BC on February 10, 2026). The database record notes that OpenAI had detected and banned van Rootselaar's account in June 2025 for activity "in furtherance of violent activities" but did not notify the RCMP because the planning was not deemed "credible or imminent."

**What these lawsuits allege:**
- OpenAI detected van Rootselaar as a violent threat in June 2025 — 8 months before the attack
- OpenAI chose not to notify law enforcement despite internal human reviewers who were alarmed
- This failure to report directly contributed to the deaths of eight people
- OpenAI's post-incident "cooperation" (sharing account info with RCMP after the shooting) was inadequate to prevent harm it could have foreseen

**Procedural note:** The seven suits are filed separately but are likely to be related/consolidated by the court. Case numbers are not yet confirmed in available public sources. These cases may ultimately be coordinated with other OpenAI wrongful death cases, though they involve a Canadian incident rather than US events. PACER access will be required to obtain full complaint text.

**Significance for the database:** This is the first U.S. civil litigation directly arising from the Tumbler Ridge shooting. It converts the Tumbler Ridge incident from a "criminal investigation" matter (RCMP probe) to an active civil litigation matter in U.S. federal court. The $1B+ claim makes this among the highest-value wrongful death actions in this litigation category.
