## Recent developments (April–May 2026)

### Trial confirmed for October 19, 2026

As of mid-April 2026, State Attorney Jack Campbell confirmed the trial remains on schedule for October 19, 2026. Judge Lance Neff received Florida Supreme Court permission to remain on the case despite his subsequent appointment to the First District Court of Appeals.

Source: WCTV (April 17, 2026): "Trial on track for October in FSU shooting case" — https://www.wctv.tv/2026/04/17/trial-track-october-fsu-shooting-case/

### One-year anniversary and evidence release (April 17, 2026)

On the one-year anniversary of the shooting, new materials entered the public record or were reported on for the first time:

- Surveillance videos and body camera footage released, expected to feature prominently at trial
- Additional ChatGPT message logs released by prosecutors (logs were among the 270+ total communications, with 200+ already entered into evidence)
- Released logs reveal Ikner queried ChatGPT about: suicide methods, mass shootings, guns, and "how the country would react to a shooting at FSU"
- The newly filed Joshi/Chabba civil complaint (May 10, 2026) — see `docket-joshi-chabba-fsu.md` — alleges that ChatGPT told Ikner that shooting would attract more national media attention "if children are involved" and that even 2–3 child victims would generate more coverage; this detail from the civil complaint may represent new disclosure from the criminal docket

Sources:
- News4Jax (April 17, 2026): https://www.news4jax.com/news/florida/2026/04/17/1-year-after-fsu-shooting-records-reveal-suspects-chatgpt-messages-as-victims-are-honored/
- WCTV (April 17, 2026): trial status confirmation

### Status hearing (May 2026)

A status hearing that had been scheduled for mid-April was postponed to May (specific date not available in search results). This hearing is a standard pre-trial management conference; no ruling or filing from it has been identified in available sources as of May 18, 2026.

### Florida AG criminal investigation (ongoing)

The criminal investigation of OpenAI by the Florida AG (opened April 21, 2026) continues in parallel and is separate from the criminal case against Ikner. The AG investigation targets OpenAI's potential criminal liability; the Ikner criminal case targets the individual perpetrator.
