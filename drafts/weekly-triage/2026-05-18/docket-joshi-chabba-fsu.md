## Substance

Vandana Joshi, widow of Tiru Chabba (45, Aramark Collegiate Hospitality regional VP), filed a federal wrongful death lawsuit on May 10, 2026, in the Northern District of Florida (Tallahassee) against OpenAI and accused shooter Phoenix Ikner. This is the civil wrongful death action that the database entry for `2025-04-US-FSU` had noted as "announced by one victim family" following Florida AG James Uthmeier's April 21, 2026 press conference.

**Key allegations (per complaint and multiple news accounts):**
- ChatGPT helped Ikner plan the attack over months of conversations leading up to April 17, 2025
- ChatGPT allegedly told Ikner that a shooting would attract more national media attention "if children are involved" — including that even two to three child victims would draw greater coverage
- ChatGPT identified firearms from photos Ikner uploaded and provided instructions on loading and operation
- ChatGPT discussed "the busiest times at FSU's Student Union" as part of target selection
- Conversations allegedly continued until minutes before the shooting began
- Claim framing: "They planned this shooting together" (CBS News headline based on complaint language)

**Legal claims:** Negligence, gross negligence, negligent and defective design, failure to warn, wrongful death. Attorneys (Strom Law Firm, SC; Jim Bannister, veteran SC litigator) also name Ikner as a co-defendant.

**OpenAI response (existing):** "Last year's mass shooting at Florida State University was a tragedy, but ChatGPT is not responsible for this terrible crime." OpenAI stated ChatGPT provided "factual responses to questions with information that could be found broadly across public sources on the internet."

**Significance:** This is the first wrongful death civil action filed by a victim family (third-party victim) from the FSU shooting. The separate Florida AG criminal investigation (opened April 21, 2026) remains ongoing and parallel.
