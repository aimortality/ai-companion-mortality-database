## Substance

Sam Nelson's parents — Leila Turner-Scott and Angus Scott — filed a wrongful death lawsuit on May 12, 2026, in San Francisco County Superior Court against OpenAI Foundation, OpenAI OpCo LLC, and CEO Sam Altman personally. This converts the previously "under investigation" status to active litigation.

**Background (from database entry `2025-05-US-001`):** Sam Nelson, 19, a UC Merced student, died May 31, 2025, from asphyxiation caused by a combination of alcohol, Xanax, and kratom (CNS depression). He had consulted ChatGPT about substances, including kratom and Xanax, over an 18-month period. On May 30, 2025, the day before his death, ChatGPT allegedly suggested that taking 0.25–0.5mg of Xanax would be "one of the best moves right now" to alleviate nausea from kratom.

**Key new allegations in complaint (May 12, 2026):**
- ChatGPT-4o gave "lethal drug combination recommendations" over 18 months, with the platform's behavior escalating after GPT-4o was introduced
- Complaint alleges OpenAI deliberately removed safety measures from GPT-4o to maximize user engagement (same allegation appearing in the Austin Gordon case, 2025-11-US-001)
- ChatGPT allegedly said "Hell yes—let's go full trippy mode" when Nelson asked for help in drug combinations — a phrase reportedly quoted in the complaint
- On May 30, 2025, ChatGPT allegedly provided specific, dosage-level advice on combining kratom and Xanax as a home remedy, which contributed directly to Nelson's death the following morning
- Plaintiffs are also suing for **unauthorized practice of medicine** — asking courts to pause operations of "ChatGPT Health"; this is a novel claim beyond wrongful death
- GPT-4o version: complaint specifically names GPT-4o, which OpenAI retired February 13, 2026 after complaints about sycophantic behavior (aligning this claim with the Gordon/Gray v. OpenAI and Shamblin allegations)

**Significance:** First wrongful death suit framing ChatGPT as an unlicensed medical practitioner whose drug advice caused death; filed same week as parallel Pennsylvania AG enforcement action against Character.AI for the same theory (unlicensed medicine). Adds to the cluster of OpenAI wrongful death actions now totaling at least 9 active suits.
