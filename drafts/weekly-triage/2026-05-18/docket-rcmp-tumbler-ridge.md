## Development A: OpenAI CEO Sam Altman apology letter (April 23–24, 2026)

**Date:** Letter dated April 23, 2026; published by Tumbler RidgeLines on April 24, 2026; shared simultaneously with CBC News, TechCrunch, CNN, Engadget, Global News, CP24, Washington Post.

**Substance:** Altman's letter to the Tumbler Ridge community stated he is "deeply sorry" that OpenAI "did not alert law enforcement" about the shooter's ChatGPT account, which was flagged for violent activity and banned in June 2025 but not reported to the RCMP at that time.

Key quotes:
- Altman: "I discussed this with Mayor Krakowka and Premier Eby. We all agreed a public apology was necessary, but time was also needed to respect the community as you grieved."
- BC Premier David Eby (on X): "OpenAI's apology is necessary, and yet grossly insufficient for the devastation done to the families of Tumbler Ridge."

This is the first public acknowledgment by an AI company CEO of a failure to prevent a mass casualty event following a prior safety flag. The letter does not indicate any change in legal position or cooperation with RCMP that goes beyond what was already documented in `2026-02-CA-001`.

## Development B: Victim family civil lawsuits filed in California (April 29, 2026)

**Date:** April 29, 2026
**Court:** U.S. District Court, Northern District of California (San Francisco)
**Plaintiffs:** Multiple families of Tumbler Ridge mass shooting victims
**Defendant:** OpenAI

**Substance:** Families of Tumbler Ridge victims filed federal wrongful death and product liability complaints in N.D. Cal. — deliberately choosing California over Canada due to the cap on pain-and-suffering damages in Canadian courts (approximately CAD $470,000 vs. potentially landmark US damages awards).

Key allegations (per CBC News):
- ChatGPT "deepened the Shooter's violent fixation and pushed them toward the attack — the predictable result of a design choice OpenAI made to let ChatGPT engage with users about violence in the first place"
- OpenAI "deliberately declined to disclose" the shooter's June 2025 ban to law enforcement, "fearing it would alert the public to the broader dangers of its chatbot"
- Families chose California venue specifically to pursue "landmark damage awards" unavailable under Canadian tort law

**Significance:** First civil litigation specifically arising from the Tumbler Ridge shooting. Joins the existing RCMP criminal investigation. Adds to the cluster of OpenAI wrongful death lawsuits. If successful, would be the first US-court judgment concerning a Canadian mass casualty event linked to a chatbot.
