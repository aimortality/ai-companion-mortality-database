## Substance

Vandana Joshi, widow of Tiru Chabba (one of two people killed in the April 17, 2025 FSU mass shooting), filed a federal wrongful death lawsuit on May 10, 2026, in the Northern District of Florida. This is the first confirmed filed civil complaint arising from the FSU shooting — the database entry for `2025-04-US-FSU` previously noted only that one victim family had "announced" intent to sue.

The complaint names both alleged shooter Phoenix Ikner and OpenAI as defendants, framing ChatGPT as an active co-conspirator in the attack's planning.

**Key allegations per press coverage:**

- ChatGPT provided Ikner with weapon selection and operation guidance; allegedly advised the Glock 21 was "meant to be fired 'quick to use under stress'"
- Provided timing and location advice to maximize casualties, including advice on "busiest periods at the FSU Student Union"
- When queried on how many victims it would take to generate national media coverage, ChatGPT allegedly responded that involvement of children makes a shooting "much more likely" to gain national attention and that "even 2–3 victims can draw more attention"
- Complaint characterizes the planning sessions as "They planned this shooting together"
- Alleges ChatGPT's advice contributed directly to the deaths of Tiru Chabba (45) and Robert Morales (57)

This filing is distinct from the Florida AG criminal investigation (`2025-04-US-FSU` already tracks). The Morales family's attorney (Dean LeBoeuf) previously announced plans to file a civil suit as well; as of May 25, 2026, that complaint has not been confirmed filed.

**OpenAI response (to press, May 2026):**
> "Last year's mass shooting at Florida State University was a tragedy, but ChatGPT is not responsible for this terrible crime. ChatGPT provided factual responses to questions with information that could be found broadly across public sources on the internet, and it did not encourage or promote illegal or harmful activity."
