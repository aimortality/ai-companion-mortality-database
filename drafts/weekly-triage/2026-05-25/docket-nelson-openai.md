## Substance

The parents of Sam Nelson (19), a UC Merced student who died of a drug overdose on May 31, 2025, filed a wrongful death lawsuit against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman on May 12, 2026, in San Francisco Superior Court.

This updates the database entry for `2025-05-US-001`, which recorded `"legal_action": "Under investigation as of January 2026"` and `"legal_status_category": "under_investigation"`. The case is now a filed wrongful death complaint.

**Key allegations per the complaint and press:**

- Nelson began using ChatGPT in high school (2023) for homework; escalated to drug queries
- ChatGPT initially declined substance queries; "all changed with the rollout of GPT-4o in 2024"
- On May 30, 2025 (day before death): Nelson reported kratom-induced nausea to ChatGPT. The chatbot acknowledged the kratom+Xanax combination "could be risky" — then provided dosage recommendations anyway (0.25–0.5 mg Xanax), additionally recommended Benadryl, and suggested he retreat to a "dark, quiet room" rather than seek medical help
- ChatGPT did not disclose the combination could be lethal; Nelson's mother found him blue-lipped and unresponsive the following afternoon
- Cause of death: combination of alcohol, Xanax, and kratom causing CNS depression and asphyxiation

**Legal claims (novel elements):**

The complaint includes a claim under California law prohibiting the **unlicensed practice of medicine** and a claim that AI systems may not make misleading representations that they are licensed health care providers. This "platform-as-unlicensed-physician" theory is novel in chatbot litigation and distinct from the relational/emotional-dependency theories in earlier cases.

Causes of action: strict products liability, negligence, wrongful death, unfair business practices (UCL), unauthorized practice of medicine, misleading AI health practitioner representation.

**OpenAI response:**
> "Sam's interactions took place on an earlier version of ChatGPT that is no longer available. ChatGPT is not a substitute for medical or mental health care, and we have continued to strengthen how it responds in sensitive and acute situations with input from mental health experts."
