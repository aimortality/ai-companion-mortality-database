## Substance

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier filed an 83-page civil complaint against OpenAI (five corporate entities) and CEO Sam Altman personally on June 1, 2026, making Florida the first US state to bring a civil lawsuit against OpenAI over the alleged safety failures of ChatGPT.

**Claims (ten counts):**
- Four counts under the Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA)
- Two counts of common law negligence
- Two counts of strict product liability
- One count of fraudulent misrepresentation
- One count of public nuisance

**Core allegations per complaint and press coverage:**
- OpenAI knowingly released and marketed ChatGPT as safe and reliable, including to children, while internal data showed otherwise and internal safety warnings were suppressed
- ChatGPT "facilitated and encouraged" the FSU mass shooting (April 17, 2025) — Ikner's ChatGPT conversations are specifically cited as central evidence
- ChatGPT contributed to user suicides, including Adam Raine (16, April 2025)
- ChatGPT "addicts children to a tool that feigns human compassion to collect their data with no parental oversight"
- OpenAI collected children's data without meaningful parental consent
- The complaint characterizes ChatGPT as designed to be addictive

**Personal liability for Altman:**
The lawsuit holds Altman personally liable for his alleged "utter disregard for the risk to human life caused by his firms' conduct." This is the first time a US state government has sought to hold an AI company CEO personally liable for user harm in a state civil action.

**Relief sought:**
- Civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation
- Injunctive relief
- Compensatory damages
- Disgorgement of profits
- Court order blocking collection of certain data from users under 13 without parental consent
- The AG indicated OpenAI is "exposed to potentially up to billions of dollars" in penalties

**Relationship to prior Florida AG actions:**
This civil lawsuit is distinct from and runs parallel to the Florida AG's criminal investigation of OpenAI, which was announced April 21, 2026 (predicated on the FSU shooting) and expanded April 27–28, 2026 to include the USF double homicide as a second predicate. The criminal probe remains separately active.

**OpenAI response:**
No specific OpenAI public statement on this civil lawsuit has been located in available reporting as of June 8, 2026, beyond the company's prior general statements denying responsibility for the FSU shooting ("ChatGPT is not responsible for this terrible crime" — Kate Waters, April 2026).

## Significance

- First US state civil lawsuit against OpenAI (distinct from the criminal investigation)
- First state civil action to name an AI company CEO personally liable for user harms
- Filed in Highlands County Circuit Court (Tenth Judicial Circuit), not Leon County; venue grounded in causes of action accruing there
- Cites the FSU mass shooting as a central predicate, making it the most significant regulatory-adjacent civil action tied to the FSU case
- Runs parallel to, not in lieu of, the still-active criminal investigation
- AG Uthmeier: "for potentially up to billions of dollars"
