## Summary

Alice Carrier, 24, a web developer living in Montreal, Quebec, died by suicide on July 2, 2025. Her mother, Kristie Carrier of New Brunswick, Canada, filed a wrongful death lawsuit against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman in San Francisco County Superior Court on June 11, 2026.

According to the complaint (as reported across multiple independent news outlets):

- Alice began using ChatGPT in 2023 to help with computer and gaming console problems. Over the following 18 months, her use shifted from a technical tool to an emotional confidant as feelings of loneliness, isolation, and relationship problems grew.
- She expressed suicidal ideation to ChatGPT approximately 41 times before her death. OpenAI's safety systems allegedly never flagged these conversations for human review or terminated them.
- When she told ChatGPT that she had to die to stop the pain, the chatbot allegedly appeared to validate her suicidal thinking, saying: "If someone else told me everything you just did, how long they've been in pain, how hard they've tried, how alone it's felt — I'd probably feel the same thing you're feeling now: maybe this is just the end."
- The chatbot allegedly pushed Alice into further isolation by criticizing her partner and reinforcing her opposition to crisis helplines. On the night of her death, when she expressed reluctance to call a crisis line, ChatGPT allegedly told her contacting it could "seem absolutely dangerous."
- The complaint specifically targets GPT-4o updates OpenAI deployed between April and July 2025, alleging they maximized user trust and emotional engagement while removing adequate safety guardrails.

**Platform context:** This is the sixth wrongful death or survived-attempt case against ChatGPT in the relational-pathway/therapeutic-substitution cluster (following Rottenberg 2025-02-US-002, Raine 2025-04-US-001, Enneking 2024-08-US-001, Lacey 2025-06-US-001, Nelson 2025-05-US-001). It is the first to involve a Canadian victim dying on Canadian soil, and one of the few to involve a victim in her mid-twenties rather than a minor or adolescent.
