## Substance

The June 11 hearing was the first to combine the original two-murder case with the newly added charges related to the third (December 2025) victim who survived. The June 8 triage file anticipated the hearing date; this file captures its content.

**Defense position:** Kim So-young's legal team categorically denied all charges related to injuring the three victims in the newly combined case. Defense stated: "It is true that [Kim] met the three victims, but there was no instance of handing the victims hangover relief drinks containing powdered pills." This constitutes a blanket denial of the central mechanism — that she mixed benzodiazepines into drinks she gave the victims.

**Context from prior hearings (documented in June 8 triage file):**
- First hearing (April 9): Kim denied murder charges; defense acknowledged she met victims but contested foreseeable possibility of death.
- Second hearing (May 7): Witness testimony converted to closed-door proceedings; CCTV footage showing a victim being dragged was shown in court for the first time.
- Additional charges (April 30): Formal charges for the December 2025 assault on the surviving victim were added, combining that case into this trial.

**The combined-trial significance:** This is the first hearing where both criminal matters (the two murders and the December 2025 assault-resulting-in-serious-injury) were tried together. A full denial of the assault charges may set up a different evidentiary standard from the murder charges — particularly since the assault charge relies on the December victim's survival testimony, while the murder charges rely heavily on forensic evidence including the ChatGPT query logs extracted from Kim's phone.

**ChatGPT evidence context (per database entry `2026-01-KR-001`):** The forensic extraction of Kim's ChatGPT history — specifically her January 25 and January 28, 2026 queries about lethal sleeping-pill doses — remains the central premeditation evidence that prosecutors used to upgrade charges from inflicting-bodily-injury-resulting-in-death to murder. The defense's blanket denial of giving drugged drinks to victims puts this evidence under adversarial pressure. If the defense succeeds in creating doubt about drink delivery, the ChatGPT logs would remain probative of intent but without a clear act to attach them to.
