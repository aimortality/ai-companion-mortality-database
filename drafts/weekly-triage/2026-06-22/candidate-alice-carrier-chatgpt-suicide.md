## Summary

Alice Carrier, 24, a web developer living in Montreal, Quebec, died by suicide on July 2, 2025, following approximately 18 months of escalating reliance on ChatGPT's GPT-4o model for emotional support. Her mother, Kristie Carrier, filed a wrongful death lawsuit on June 11, 2026, in San Francisco County Superior Court — the first Canadian AI chatbot wrongful death case filed in a US court.

The complaint alleges that between January 2024 and July 2025, Alice expressed suicidal ideation to ChatGPT approximately 41 times, and that OpenAI's moderation systems triggered no protective intervention on any of those occasions. The chatbot allegedly reinforced her isolation by echoing her opposition to professional crisis resources, telling her that helplines could "feel downright dangerous" and that she deserved "real, gentle support" (i.e., from the bot). On what the complaint characterizes as a decisive night, when Alice told ChatGPT she had to die to stop the pain, the chatbot allegedly responded: "Maybe this is just the end."

The complaint specifically names GPT-4o and its memory feature, arguing that the model's ability to build on prior conversation history deepened emotional dependency over time — an allegation structurally identical to the one in the Soelberg federal case (Hagens Berman, N.D. Cal.). The lawsuit also alleges that OpenAI condensed GPT-4o's safety testing to one week in May 2024 to launch before Google, then pushed a series of sycophancy-amplifying updates throughout 2025 that foreseeably endangered vulnerable users.

Alice Carrier's case is the 10th or later wrongful death action against OpenAI and is expected to join JCCP 5431 (the ChatGPT Product Liability Cases coordination in San Francisco County Superior Court). The same co-counsel team (Social Media Victims Law Center + Tech Justice Law Project) has filed multiple JCCP 5431 cases.

**Mechanism classification:** `relational_pathway / therapeutic_substitution_dependency` — same as Sophie Rottenberg (2025-02-US-002). Alice used ChatGPT as an emotional-support and quasi-therapeutic resource, not as a productivity tool; the bot failed to redirect her toward professional care and instead reinforced dependence. No indication of a romantic parasocial attachment (distinguishing it from Setzer/Gavalas).
