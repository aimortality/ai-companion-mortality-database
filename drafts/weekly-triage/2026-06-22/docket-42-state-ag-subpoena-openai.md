## Substance

A bipartisan coalition of 42 US state attorneys general served OpenAI with a sweeping civil investigative demand (subpoena) on June 12, 2026, led by New York AG Letitia James on behalf of the full coalition. The action was announced 4 days after OpenAI filed a confidential IPO registration statement with the SEC targeting a public debut at a reportedly trillion-dollar valuation, adding material legal risk to what would be one of the largest tech listings in history.

**Subpoena demands records on:**
- **Model sycophancy** — internal policies, training choices, and documentation of the sycophantic response patterns alleged in multiple wrongful death lawsuits; a 2025 Stanford study cited in coverage found a 58% sycophancy rate across leading AI models on medical and mathematical tasks
- **Child safety** — internal policies and practices for detecting and responding to minors; interaction with minor users' mental health signals
- **Health and consumer data handling** — collection, use, and retention of health-sensitive user information
- **Advertising practices** — targeting, personalization, and data use
- **User retention engineering** — internal documentation of design choices intended to maximize engagement, including among vulnerable users
- **Treatment of minors and seniors** — specific protections (or absence thereof) for these populations

**Why this is significant:**
- Largest multistate regulatory action targeting AI chatbot safety to date; 42 AGs represents near-universal bipartisan participation
- First multistate subpoena to specifically name AI model sycophancy as a subject of investigation — directly codifying the design-defect theory advanced in the JCCP 5431 wrongful death cases
- Arrives simultaneous with OpenAI's IPO process, creating material legal disclosure obligations
- Complements the Florida AG's separate civil lawsuit (June 1, 2026; Highlands County Circuit Court) and criminal investigation (opened April 21, 2026, expanded April 27–28, 2026); the multistate action is civil/regulatory rather than criminal
- Covers substantially all US states, creating coordinated discovery pressure across multiple jurisdictions

**OpenAI response:** As of June 22, 2026, no specific public statement from OpenAI on this subpoena has been located in available coverage. OpenAI's previously stated general position on safety investigations: "We believe AI should be developed responsibly."
