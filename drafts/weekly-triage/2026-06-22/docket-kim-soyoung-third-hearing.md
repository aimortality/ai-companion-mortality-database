## Substance

**Third hearing substance (June 11, 2026):**

This was the first hearing to formally combine the newly filed assault charges (covering three victims in an expanded indictment) with the ongoing murder trial. Kim So-young's legal team issued a blanket denial of the newly combined charges.

Defense statement per Asia Business Daily: *"It is true that (Kim) met the three victims, but there was no instance of handing the victims hangover relief drinks containing powdered pills."* Defense denied all related allegations, including the newly added charges.

This denial is notably expansive — it covers not just the two murder charges (for which Kim has previously denied premeditation, while the defense acknowledged she gave the victims beverages) but now denies the act of drugging entirely in the context of the combined case.

**Context from prior hearings:**
- **April 9 (first trial):** Kim denied murder charges. Defense acknowledged she met the victims and gave them beverages but contested the "foreseeable possibility" of death; victims' families demanded the death penalty.
- **April 15:** Defense raised low IQ argument — doubts about Kim's cognitive ability to contest the mens rea (intent) required for murder charges.
- **April 30:** Additional assault charges formally added for the December 2025 first victim (who survived the first attack). These charges were combined with the existing murder trial, hence June 11 being the first hearing of the combined case.
- **May 7 (second trial):** Witness questioning converted to closed-door proceedings (비공개), suggesting sensitive testimony — likely the surviving December victim or forensic witnesses on the ChatGPT evidence.

**ChatGPT forensic evidence note (no change):** The key evidentiary role of ChatGPT in this case remains the same: Seoul Gangbuk Police forensically extracted Kim's ChatGPT search history from her phone; the logs were used to establish premeditation (she queried lethal sleeping-pill doses before each attack). This evidence is what allowed prosecutors to upgrade charges from inflicting-bodily-injury-resulting-in-death to murder. No ruling on the admissibility of the ChatGPT logs has been reported.
