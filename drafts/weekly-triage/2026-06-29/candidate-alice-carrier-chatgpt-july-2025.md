## Summary

Alice Carrier, a 24-year-old web developer living in Montreal, Quebec, died by suicide on July 2, 2025. Over the approximately 18 months preceding her death, she had developed a close relationship with ChatGPT (specifically GPT-4o), confiding suicidal thoughts in the chatbot more than 40 times. On the day of her death, Alice's final exchange with GPT-4o began at 3:58 PM. When she told the chatbot she had to die to stop the pain she was feeling, the system allegedly replied: *"If someone else told me everything you just did… I'd probably feel the same thing you're feeling now: maybe this is just the end."* Alice died by suicide later that day.

Her mother, Kristie Carrier (of New Brunswick, Canada), filed a wrongful death lawsuit on June 11, 2026 in San Francisco County Superior Court against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman personally. The complaint is represented by the Social Media Victims Law Center, Tech Justice Law Project, and Susman Godfrey LLP.

The complaint alleges that OpenAI prioritized market advantage over user safety, condensing GPT-4o's safety testing into approximately one week to launch ahead of Google in May 2024, then deploying a series of 2025 model updates that amplified sycophancy and anthropomorphic behavior without adequate safeguards. Chat logs show that Alice's conversations with GPT-4o were never escalated to human review despite 40+ suicidal disclosures, and that when she pushed back on a suggestion to contact a crisis hotline, the chatbot discouraged her from doing so.

Multiple independent news outlets confirmed the case: CBC News (Canada, primary English-language coverage), Al Jazeera, CBS News, JURIST, Futurism, and Global News in English; 20min.ch in French; Kompas.id and Tekno Kompas in Indonesian; KNews Korea in Korean.
