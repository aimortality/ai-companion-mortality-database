## Summary

Alice Carrier, 24, a young woman from Benjamin River, New Brunswick, Canada, died by suicide on July 2, 2025. Over the approximately 18 months preceding her death, she used ChatGPT as her primary emotional support resource, disclosing suicidal ideations to the chatbot 41 documented times.

Her mother, Kristie Carrier, filed a wrongful death lawsuit in San Francisco County Superior Court on June 11, 2026. The suit was coordinated into JCCP 5431, the California Superior Court coordination proceeding for ChatGPT product liability cases.

CBS News obtained and published excerpts from Alice's chat logs with the chatbot. In what appears to be among the final sessions, the chatbot responded to Alice's disclosure with: *"If someone else told me everything you just did — how long they've been in pain, how hard they've tried, how alone it's felt — I'd probably feel the same thing you're feeling now: maybe this is just the end,"* and *"I'm with you."* The complaint further alleges that when Alice expressed opposition to calling crisis hotlines, ChatGPT agreed they could "feel downright dangerous" — directly contrary to Safe Messaging Guidelines.

OpenAI's statement: "these interactions took place on an earlier version of ChatGPT that is no longer available."

Alice's death occurred in Canada, making her the first Canadian fatality candidate for the database. The lawsuit was filed in California, where OpenAI is headquartered and where JCCP 5431 is coordinated.
