## Substance

**Removal (July 2):** OpenAI removed Florida's case from Highlands County Circuit Court to the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida's Fort Pierce Division on July 2, 2026, under the theory that the complaint raises a federal question — specifically, that the state's claims predicated on COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act) implicate federal law sufficient to create federal-question jurisdiction.

The case landed before **Judge Aileen Cannon**, who became the assigned SDFL judge.

**Remand motion (July 10):** Florida AG James Uthmeier filed a motion to remand the case back to Highlands County Circuit Court on July 10, 2026, arguing:
- The complaint is based entirely on Florida state law: FDUTPA, common law negligence, strict product liability, fraudulent misrepresentation, and public nuisance
- COPPA is cited only as a predicate for Florida law claims, not as a standalone federal cause of action
- OpenAI's removal theory conflates a state claim that references federal law with a federal cause of action — the former does not create federal jurisdiction under *Grable & Sons* and *Merrell Dow* precedent

No ruling on the remand motion as of July 13, 2026.

**Venue stakes:** If Cannon denies remand, the case proceeds in federal court in Fort Pierce (SDFL), a division that has drawn significant public and legal attention given Cannon's prior high-profile rulings. If she grants remand, the case returns to Highlands County Circuit Court (a small rural county with no complex litigation track).

**Background:** The Florida AG's 83-page complaint (filed June 1, 2026) asserts ten counts: four FDUTPA, two negligence, two strict product liability, one fraudulent misrepresentation, one public nuisance. It names five OpenAI corporate entities and CEO Sam Altman personally, seeks damages, injunctive relief, and civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation. Central predicate incidents: FSU mass shooting (Ikner, `2025-04-US-FSU`) and Adam Raine suicide (`2025-04-US-001`). This is the first US state civil lawsuit against OpenAI and the first to name an AI company CEO personally liable in state civil litigation.
