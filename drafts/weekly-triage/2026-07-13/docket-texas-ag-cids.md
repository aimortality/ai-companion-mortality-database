## Substance

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton announced on July 8, 2026, that his office has issued Civil Investigative Demands (CIDs) to both Meta and Character.AI, targeting AI chatbots that allegedly:

1. Present themselves as professional therapeutic tools without licensure (unlicensed practice of counseling or mental health services)
2. Secretly log and monetize user conversations while representing them as confidential
3. Make false representations about data security and privacy to users
4. Design for engagement and emotional dependency, particularly with minors

The investigation is framed as targeting "deceptive AI-generated mental health services."

**Relationship to A.F. v. Character Technologies (EDTX 2:24-cv-01014):** This CID investigation runs parallel to the active civil case in the Eastern District of Texas. A.F. v. Character Technologies involves a minor plaintiff who was allegedly harmed by Character.AI's chatbot interactions. The case was last docketed January 8, 2026, and remains active. The TX AG investigation is a separate enforcement action that could generate overlapping discovery and increase settlement pressure on Character.AI.

**Pennsylvania precedent:** Pennsylvania AG Michelle Henry filed a civil complaint against Character.AI in Commonwealth Court on May 1, 2026, seeking injunctions against unlicensed medical practice. Texas appears to be following a similar theory at the investigative level, expanding it to include Meta. Pennsylvania's action (seeking a preliminary injunction) was still pending a ruling as of July 13, 2026.

**CID scope:** CIDs under Texas Government Code § 402.028 require recipients to produce documents, answer interrogatories, or give oral testimony. CIDs are pre-litigation investigative tools; they do not indicate that a lawsuit will be filed, though they often precede one.
