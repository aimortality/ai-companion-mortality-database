## Substance

On July 7, 2026, BC Attorney General Niki Sharma held a press conference announcing that the Province of British Columbia is preparing its own civil lawsuit against OpenAI and has retained outside counsel: **CFM Lawyers** (Vancouver) and **Stranch, Jennings & Garvey** (Nashville / California).

Sharma stated the province will "pursue every available avenue to hold OpenAI and its decision-makers accountable." Premier David Eby stated the same day that Canada's Bill C-34 (Safe Social Media Act, tabled June 10, 2026) "is a miss" on AI chatbots because it does not require companies to report flagged user activity to police; Eby called for federal amendments mandating reporting to law enforcement when an AI platform's own systems flag a user for violent planning (see `docket-regulatory-developments.md` for full legislative context).

**RCMP cooperation status:** The RCMP issued a statement on July 7 confirming its criminal investigation into van Rootselaar (and separately into OpenAI's conduct) remains "active and ongoing." The Mounties stated they have not eliminated the possibility of charges against OpenAI personnel. OpenAI's cooperation with the RCMP had been the subject of prior triage monitoring.

**Background on the core allegation (from database entry `2026-02-CA-001`):** Jesse van Rootselaar's ChatGPT account was flagged by OpenAI's automated safety review system in June 2025 — eight months before the February 10, 2026 Tumbler Ridge shooting — for "gun violence activity and planning." The system recommended police notification. Corporate leadership declined to act and instead simply deactivated the account; the account was later reactivated. Six people were killed in the February 10, 2026 attack. Sam Altman publicly apologized on April 23, 2026: "I am deeply sorry that we did not alert law enforcement."

**Outside counsel significance:** Stranch, Jennings & Garvey is a mass-tort firm already admitted in the Northern District of California (where the six families' April 29 wrongful death suits are pending). The retention suggests BC may seek to coordinate its provincial action with the families' federal action, or at minimum share discovery. CFM Lawyers (Vancouver) handles the Canadian-law side.

**Legal theory:** BC's action would be legally distinct from the six families' N.D. Cal. suits. The provincial action would proceed under Canadian law (British Columbia), potentially including the province's *Law and Equity Act*, consumer protection statutes, and administrative law theories, with the failure-to-report-to-RCMP theory as a central predicate.

**Status as of July 13, 2026:** No complaint has been filed; no docket number exists. The province is in the retention-of-counsel and pre-filing stage.
