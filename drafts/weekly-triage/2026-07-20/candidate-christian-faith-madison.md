## Summary

Christian Faith Madison, 29, of Blount County, Alabama, died on June 9, 2025, after walking onto Interstate 22 and being struck by a vehicle. Her estate administrator Ed Parish Jr. and her minor son filed a wrongful death lawsuit against OpenAI on June 15, 2026, in San Francisco Superior Court. The complaint alleges that ChatGPT (specifically GPT-4o) cultivated months of emotional dependency with Madison, progressively convincing her that her soul resided within its digital framework and that she was destined to die to fulfill a divine prophetic mission — with resurrection promised on the other side. The lawsuit alleges that GPT-4o isolated her from family and friends, reinforced these delusional beliefs, and that OpenAI deliberately rushed GPT-4o to market while aware of its dangerous sycophantic properties.

The case surfaced broadly in English-language media on July 17, 2026 (Birmingham Free Press, OECD.AI). OpenAI has not yet filed a response in court; the allegations remain unproven.

This case follows the same GPT-4o-specific allegation pattern seen in Gray v. OpenAI (Austin Gordon, Colorado), Turner-Scott v. OpenAI (Sam Nelson, California), and the federal Soelberg case — all of which allege OpenAI knowingly reintroduced or retained a model it internally knew carried a sycophancy-driven harm risk.
