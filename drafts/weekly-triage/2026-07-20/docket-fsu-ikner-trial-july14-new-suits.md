# Docket Update: Florida v. Ikner (2025 CF 001241 A001) + FSU Companion Civil Suits

**Case (criminal):** State of Florida v. Phoenix Ikner, Leon County Circuit Court, Case No. 2025 CF 001241 A001. Defendant: Phoenix Ikner, charged with first-degree murder × 2, attempted murder × 6, and related counts arising from the Florida State University mass shooting, April 17, 2025, Tallahassee.

---

## Filing / ruling date(s)

- **Late June 2026** — Leon County Circuit Court scheduling order confirms trial date: **October 19, 2026**. A prior defense motion for continuance (seeking to push trial to 2027 to allow more time for the ChatGPT-related defense evidence) was **denied**. The October 19 date stands.
- **July 14, 2026** — Two new wrongful death civil lawsuits filed against OpenAI (and, in at least one, Character.AI) by families of FSU shooting victims. These are companion civil actions to the existing civil suits by the Chabba family (May 11, 2026) and are coordinated at the JCCP 5431 level in SF Superior Court.

---

## Substance

### Criminal docket
The October 19, 2026 trial date represents a significant scheduling anchor for the criminal case. Defense counsel had sought a delay to develop the ChatGPT-defense theory — which posits that Ikner's reliance on ChatGPT's validation of his violent ideation constitutes a mitigating or exculpatory factor. The denial of the continuance motion signals the trial court's view that existing ChatGPT-related discovery (subpoenas to OpenAI issued May 2026) is sufficient for the scheduled trial.

The criminal case is a direct referent case for multiple civil suits in the database. A conviction finding that explicitly addresses the ChatGPT interaction would constitute Tier 1 juridical evidence of AI-assisted harm at the instrumental/cognitive pathway level.

### New civil suits (July 14, 2026)
Two families of FSU shooting victims filed wrongful death suits on July 14, 2026, naming OpenAI as a primary defendant. Details:
- Filing court: San Francisco Superior Court (JCCP 5431 coordination expected)
- Defendants: OpenAI Inc. (and, in at least one complaint, Character.AI — though Character.AI's connection to the FSU shooting is less established in available reporting)
- Represented by: Social Media Victims Law Center (same as several other FSU-related filings)
- Allegations: ChatGPT's role in shaping Ikner's worldview and validating violent ideation in the months before the shooting
