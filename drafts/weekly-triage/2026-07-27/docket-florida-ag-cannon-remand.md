## Substance

**Background:** Florida AG James Uthmeier filed the first US state civil lawsuit against OpenAI on June 1, 2026, in Highlands County Circuit Court. OpenAI removed the case to the Southern District of Florida. The case was assigned to Judge Aileen M. Cannon (who became nationally prominent as the presiding judge in the classified-documents case against former President Trump before it was dismissed in 2024).

**Remand motion:** The Florida AG filed a motion to remand the case back to state court. The AG's position is that OpenAI's removal was "frivolous" and that COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act) does not create a federal question sufficient to justify removal. The underlying legal question: does the AG's COPPA-related claim (blocking children's data collection without parental consent) "arise under" federal law for purposes of 28 U.S.C. § 1331, or is the Florida FDUTPA/common-law complaint purely a state-law matter?

**Briefing complete:** The FL AG filed its reply brief on or around July 10, 2026. As of July 27, no ruling from Judge Cannon has been published.

**July 22 status report:** A status report was due July 22 (within this triage window). Its content is not publicly available as of July 27.

**If remanded:** The case returns to Highlands County Circuit Court, where the AG originally filed it. OpenAI would likely face different procedural posture and could not immediately appeal the remand order to the circuit court (remand orders are generally not immediately appealable under 28 U.S.C. § 1447(d), with narrow exceptions).

**If not remanded:** The case proceeds in SDFL before Judge Cannon. OpenAI would likely file a Rule 12 motion to dismiss targeting the novel product liability and FDUTPA theories.

**Parallel criminal investigation:** The Florida AG's criminal investigation of OpenAI (announced April 21, 2026, predicated on FSU shooting; expanded to USF double homicide April 27–28) remains separately active and is not affected by the civil case venue dispute.
