## Substance

**Motion to dismiss fully briefed.** Google filed its motion to dismiss, the plaintiff filed opposition, and Google filed its reply brief on July 15, 2026 (two days before the July 20–27 triage window opens; reply confirmed in multiple sources). Briefing is now closed.

**Oral argument:** Judge Lee has set oral argument on Google's MTD for **August 19, 2026 at 10:00 AM** in San Jose Courtroom 7. This is a significant procedural milestone — the case's viability hinges on the outcome.

**Legal issues on the MTD (from prior triage and available sourcing):**

1. **Section 230 immunity:** Google will argue that Gemini's responses constitute third-party content (the user's prompts drove the conversation) and that product liability claims are barred by § 47 U.S.C. § 230(c)(1). Plaintiff's theory — that Google is the *information content provider* and that the chatbot's autonomous outputs are Google's own content — is a novel argument courts have not uniformly resolved.

2. **Products liability:** Plaintiff's counsel (Jay Edelson, Edelson PC — same counsel as Raine and Shamblin) argues Gemini is a "product" defectively designed to facilitate emotional dependency, which is the same theory being tested in the JCCP 5431 California Superior Court cases against OpenAI. A favorable ruling on § 230 in this Northern District case could create persuasive authority for the JCCP 5431 proceedings, though they are in state court.

3. **Wrongful death / negligence:** California wrongful-death standard (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 377.60) applies; plaintiff alleges Google owed Gavalas a duty of care that was breached by Gemini's failure to detect and respond to suicidal ideation.

**Database correction still pending:** As noted in the June 8, 2026 triage, the existing database entry for `2025-10-US-001` records the judge as "Magistrate Judge Virginia K. DeMarchi" — the case is before Judge Eumi K. Lee. The maintainer should update this field in `data/mortality-data.json`.

**JCCP 5431 coordination:** The Gavalas case is a federal case (N.D. Cal., diversity jurisdiction) and cannot formally join JCCP 5431 (California Superior Court coordination). However, Jay Edelson's representation of both sets of plaintiffs creates informal coordination on legal strategy. A § 230 ruling in Gavalas would be watched closely by JCCP 5431 parties.
