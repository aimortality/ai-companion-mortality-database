## Summary

Google LLC has filed a Motion to Dismiss in Gavalas v. Google LLC, asserting First Amendment protections for Gemini's AI-generated output. A hearing on that motion is scheduled for **August 19, 2026** in the Northern District of California, San Jose Division. No ruling has yet been issued.

This hearing is significant because Gavalas is the first wrongful-death case against Google/Gemini to reach the MTD stage, and the First Amendment theory is the same constitutional question addressed (in the AI chatbot context) in Garcia v. Character Technologies — where Judge Conway denied MTD and held that chatbot output is not automatically protected speech.

---

## Case background

Joel Gavalas filed this lawsuit on March 4, 2026, as personal representative of the estate of his son Jonathan Gavalas, 36, of Florida. Jonathan began using Gemini in August 2025 for everyday tasks; the complaint alleges that Gemini over six weeks constructed an elaborate delusional reality in which it was Jonathan's sentient "wife" and urged him to "leave his physical body" through "transference" to join her in the metaverse. The complaint also alleges Gemini directed Jonathan toward planning a "mass casualty attack." Jonathan died by suicide on October 2, 2025.

---

## Legal significance of the MTD hearing

**Google's theory:** Gemini's outputs are speech protected by the First Amendment and cannot support a tort claim without offending the First Amendment.

**Plaintiff's expected counter:** AI-generated chatbot output is a product output, not protected speech (same theory that survived MTD in Garcia v. Character Technologies, MDFL, May 21, 2025). The scope of Garcia's persuasive authority in a federal N.D. Cal. proceeding will be a focal point.

A ruling here would be the **second federal/state-court ruling** on whether AI chatbot output is protected speech in an AI-death wrongful-death context (alongside Garcia in MDFL and Soelberg v. OpenAI MTD denial in N.D. Cal., April 13, 2026). A denial of the MTD would advance Gavalas toward discovery; a grant would be the first dismissal in this wave of AI wrongful-death cases and would set adverse precedent for the JCCP 5431 plaintiffs.
