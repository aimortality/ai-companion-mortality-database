# Docket update: RCMP / van Rootselaar — BC province announces legal action against OpenAI

**Related case:** `2026-02-CA-001` — Jesse van Rootselaar / Tumbler Ridge mass shooting (February 10, 2026)
**Development date:** July 7, 2026
**Development type:** Government legal-action announcement — pre-filing (no formal complaint yet)

---

## Summary

On July 7, 2026, British Columbia Attorney General Niki Sharma announced that the provincial government has retained legal counsel in both BC and California to pursue potential legal action against OpenAI over the February 10, 2026 Tumbler Ridge mass shooting.

This is the first time a Canadian provincial government has moved toward civil litigation against an AI company over a specific harm event. As of August 3, 2026, no formal complaint has been filed; the province is in the pre-filing investigation and strategy phase.

**BC retained counsel:**
- **CFM Lawyers** (Vancouver, BC) — BC-side representation
- **Stranch, Jennings & Garvey** (California) — US-side representation for potential California filing

**AG Niki Sharma's statement:** The province is pursuing "all legal avenues to hold OpenAI and its decision-makers accountable for its documented failure to notify law enforcement regarding explicit, flagged threats made by the perpetrator on the company's ChatGPT platform."

---

## Background (from `2026-02-CA-001` canonical record)

On February 10, 2026, 18-year-old Jesse van Rootselaar killed their mother and half-brother (age 11) at their home, then proceeded to Tumbler Ridge Secondary School, where five students (ages 11–13) and one educational assistant were killed and 27 others wounded, before dying by self-inflicted gunshot. The incident is the worst school mass shooting in Canadian history.

Prior to the attack, internal OpenAI safety teams flagged van Rootselaar's ChatGPT account for violent content violating platform policies. According to publicly disclosed information, the company considered notifying law enforcement but chose not to do so. Van Rootselaar's account was subsequently banned.

In April 2026, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman issued a public letter of apology to the Tumbler Ridge community. Families of victims filed separate wrongful-death lawsuits against OpenAI in April 2026 (NPR coverage: https://www.npr.org/2026/04/29/nx-s1-5798896/tumbler-ridge-mass-shooting-chat-gpt-lawsuit).

---

## Legal theory

The province's anticipated claim centers on OpenAI's failure to report known violent threats to law enforcement — a theory distinct from the product-liability and negligent-design theories in most US wrongful-death cases. BC's complaint, when filed, would likely allege:

1. OpenAI had actual knowledge of specific violent threats (internal safety flags)
2. OpenAI had a duty to report or a duty of care to potential victims
3. OpenAI's decision not to notify police was a proximate cause of the shootings

This is conceptually analogous to the Florida AG's criminal investigation (announced April 21, 2026) but is a Canadian provincial civil action in parallel.

**Jurisdictional note:** No formal filing has occurred; the California-licensed counsel suggests the action may be filed in US federal or state court (diversity jurisdiction) or in coordination with JCCP 5431, rather than in Canadian courts. Confirm jurisdiction when complaint is filed.
