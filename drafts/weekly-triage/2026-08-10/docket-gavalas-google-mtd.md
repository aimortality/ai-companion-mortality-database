## Substance

**Background recap:** Jonathan Gavalas died October 2025 in Florida. His family filed suit in N.D. Cal. (San Jose Division) on March 4, 2026, alleging that Gavalas's prolonged interaction with Google's Gemini AI contributed to or caused his death. The case was filed in federal court in California, the location of Google's headquarters. See `data/mortality-data.json` incident `2025-10-US-001` for full case record.

**Google's Motion to Dismiss:** Google has filed a motion to dismiss the complaint. Reporting and court monitoring services indicate Google is asserting a **First Amendment defense** — characterizing Gemini's outputs as protected speech or ideas, arguing the platform cannot be held liable for the content of its responses on constitutional grounds. This is the same broad legal theory that Character.AI has raised in the Garcia and A.F. cases (MDFL and EDTX respectively), though the factual posture differs (Gemini is a general-purpose assistant; Character.AI cases involve roleplay personas).

**CMC continuance:** The parties filed a joint stipulation to continue the initial Case Management Conference, which has been reset to September 16, 2026. The joint continuance suggests both sides are treating the August 19 MTD hearing as dispositive for case direction.

**Significance:** If Judge Lee denies the MTD, the case proceeds to discovery and Google would be required to produce documents regarding Gemini's training, safety reviews, and any internal knowledge of risks. If she grants the MTD, the plaintiff would likely amend the complaint. A ruling upholding the First Amendment defense in a Gemini wrongful-death case would have significant precedential implications across all tracked AI liability dockets, including the JCCP 5431 ChatGPT coordination in California.
