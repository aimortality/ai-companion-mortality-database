AI Companion Mortality Database

Public database documenting deaths in which AI chatbot interaction was alleged as a contributing factor.
All cases verified through court documents, news sources, or government acknowledgment.
Last updated: August 21, 2026 | Deaths: 35 | Incidents: 24 | Period: March 2023 — August 2026

This database documents fatalities where interaction with conversational AI systems was alleged as a contributing factor—through lawsuits, family statements, investigations, or government inquiries. Between March 2023 and August 2026, we identified 35 fatalities across 24 incidents (occurrences of harm) involving 6 platforms; in 2 of these, victims survived rather than died. This includes 18 AI users who died and 17 third-party victims killed by AI users. Cases were verified through court documents, multiple independent news sources, or official government acknowledgment.

The data reveals concerning patterns: 29% of victims were minors (ages 11–17), and 2025–2026 account for 19 incidents with fatalities—exceeding all previous years combined. Fatalities where ChatGPT use was cited (n=29, including 16 third-party victims) exceeded those of all other platforms combined. The ECRI Institute ranked AI chatbot misuse as the #1 Health Technology Hazard for 2026. On April 21, 2026, Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier opened a criminal investigation into OpenAI over ChatGPT's alleged role in the April 2025 FSU mass shooting—the first US state criminal probe directly targeting an AI company over a mass-casualty event, later expanded to include the April 2026 USF double homicide as a second predicate. On June 1, 2026, the same office filed a separate civil lawsuit against five OpenAI entities and CEO Sam Altman personally—the first US state civil suit against OpenAI and the first to name an AI company CEO personally liable for user harms. The first documented DeepSeek-involved homicide (Roberts/Shellis, Wales, October 2025) added an 8th platform and the first non-Western corporate AI to the registry following a UK criminal conviction in March 2026. Unlike speculative discussions of AI existential risk, this work focuses on documented cases where AI interaction was cited as a factor.

Key Findings:
  • 35 total fatalities across 24 incidents, 6 AI platforms with documented deaths (Mar 2023–Aug 2026): 18 AI users + 17 third-party victims
  • 29% of victims were minors (10 of 35), with ages ranging from 11 to 17 years
  • 19 fatal incidents occurred in 2025–2026, exceeding all previous years combined
  • ChatGPT leads in absolute numbers (29 fatalities: 13 users + 16 third-party victims)
  • Five alleged instrumental-pathway incidents documented: FSU (Apr 2025), Roberts/Shellis Wales (Oct 2025), Tumbler Ridge (Feb 2026), USF (Apr 2026), Kim Seoul (Jan–Feb 2026)
  • Zero documented deaths for Claude (Anthropic) and Replika platforms
  • ECRI Institute ranked AI chatbot misuse as #1 Health Technology Hazard for 2026
  • Regulatory landscape broadening in 2026: California JCCP 5431 coordinated seven OpenAI wrongful-death cases (Feb); Pennsylvania AG filed first state-AG enforcement framing chatbot conduct as unlicensed practice of medicine (May); Canada's Privacy Commissioner found OpenAI violated PIPEDA in training ChatGPT (May); a 42-state attorney general coalition led by New York AG Letitia James served OpenAI with a civil investigative demand covering sycophancy, child safety, health data, advertising, and engagement mechanics (June 12)

Important context: This database documents cases where AI chatbot interaction has been alleged as a contributing factor—through lawsuits, investigations, government inquiries, or family statements. The database makes no independent claims of causation. Many of these cases involve individuals with pre-existing vulnerabilities, and chatbot interaction was one of multiple factors cited.

Cases exist on a spectrum of accountability. In most, allegations remain unresolved. However, some have progressed beyond accusation: one case resulted in a settlement (Setzer/Character.AI-Google, January 2026); one company acknowledged prior detection and changed its policies (OpenAI/van Rootselaar); a landmark court ruling classified chatbot output as a product rather than protected speech (Garcia v. Character Technologies, May 2025); seven OpenAI wrongful-death cases were judicially coordinated at the California state level (JCCP 5431, February 2026); a state Attorney General filed first-of-its-kind enforcement framing chatbot conduct as unlicensed practice of medicine (Pennsylvania v. Character Technologies, May 2026); and a foreign privacy regulator made the first formal finding that OpenAI violated national law (Canada's Privacy Commissioner, PIPEDA #2026-002, May 2026). These distinctions matter and are noted per case below.

Billions of AI chatbot interactions occur annually without documented harm. Platforms with zero documented cases (Claude, Replika) are included to demonstrate that design choices and safety-first approaches produce measurably different outcomes.
